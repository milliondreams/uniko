# LongMemEval — 11-question slice (2026-05-17)

Manual benchmark across 5 SSU + 3 SSA + 3 MS questions. Numbers below are
verbatim from the bench output JSON; nothing is averaged across reruns.

Source files (preserved on disk):
- `data/lme_q2_results.json` — 5 SSU
- `data/lme_6nonssu_results.json` — 3 SSA + 3 MS, **no reranker**
- `data/lme_6nonssu_rerank_results.json` — same 6 non-SSU questions, **reranker enabled** (post-hoc rerun via `--reuse`)

### Failure modes

- **SSA (3 questions):** session-level retrieval is perfect (R@5 = 1.000 on all 3) but the answer text isn't in the top-5 chunks of the retrieved session on 2 of 3. The Phase-1 gate's `context_contains_answer` metric measures chunk-level containment, not session-level recall. This is a **chunking-strategy** issue upstream of the recall cascade — the reranker rerun (next section) confirmed it: reranking can't fix what chunking didn't capture.
- **MS (3 questions):** incomplete recall of evidence sessions on 2 of 3. Q1 (`0a995998`) got all 3 evidence sessions; Q2 (`6d550036`) got 2 of 4; Q3 (`gpt4_59c863d7`) got 2 of 4 AND the answer chunk isn't in the retrieved set.
- **SSU:** all perfect; not informative for further iteration.

### Phase-1 gate

Phase-1 gate threshold is `context_contains_answer ≥ 90%`. This 11-question slice produces **72.7%** — would **FAIL** the gate. Drivers are SSA (33%) and the worse MS question; SSU on its own is 100%.

### What the reranker did and didn't fix

- **Did not fix any SSA `contains` failure** — those are chunking-side, not ranking-side.
- **Did not fix `gpt4_59c863d7` `contains` failure** despite recovering all evidence sessions — the answer chunk still isn't in the top-5 of those sessions.
- **Helped one MS recall** by pulling additional evidence sessions into the top 5 via cross-encoder scoring.
- **Hurt one MS recall** by sharpening top-1 relevance at the expense of diverse-evidence selection — classic cross-encoder pitfall on multi-document QA.

### The headline finding

**NLP is ~10% of total ingestion time. Entity upsert (the per-entity
sequential commit loop in `dedup::upsert_entities`) is 53%.**

This inverts the framing we've been using when discussing perf
optimizations. The NLP/SRL parallelization work (cross-message
batching, blog's Option 2) would shave at most ~10% off ingestion.
The 5× larger lever is **reducing per-entity DB commit count in
`dedup`** — batch all entity upserts of a message into one transaction
instead of one transaction per entity.

A rough projection: if dedup were refactored to do one batched commit
per message (matching how `obs_step` already does
`batch_create_nodes` + `batch_create_edges_fast`), entity_step time
could drop from ~470s to ~150s (NLP + a single batched DB write per
message). That would cut average ingest time from 12.4 min to ~7 min,
a 40-45% wall-clock saving — much bigger than the NLP optimizations.

### Observations on the ingestion numbers

- **SSA questions have the longest ingest time (15.7 min avg)** despite having fewer sessions than SSU (45-48 vs 50-53). The driver is entity count — SSA messages have ~30% more entities per turn (`avg_ents/avg_turns` ≈ 7.74 vs 6.90), which drives more graph writes during the per-entity upsert loop in `dedup::upsert_entities`.
- **MS questions ingest fastest (9.5 min avg)**: fewer entities per turn and one of the three (`6d550036`) was paired with a fast partner in batch-1 of the non-SSU run.
- **Single-process GPU FP32 baseline was 6 min/question for ingest** (see prior `lme_fp32_single.json`). q=2 nearly doubles per-question ingest time due to GPU contention — confirming the GPU is the bottleneck, not CPU or disk.
- **Consolidation time (5-6 min avg) is independent of model parallelism** — it's CPU+DB work clustering ~6,500 observations into ~2,750 facts per question. Roughly constant per observation count.
- **Query time has a long tail** (1.76 s to 13.49 s). The slow ones are when the per-question recall cascade lands while another question is also querying — RecallConfig embedder calls and reranker setup compete on GPU.

## Honest caveats

- **n = 11 is small.** The 3-question slices in SSA and MS each have huge per-question variance — flipping one R@5 between 0.5 and 1.0 swings the category average by 0.17. Don't treat these as load-bearing measurements for choosing defaults.
- **All questions are the first N from the Phase-1 set** (sorted by question_id within the dataset). No category-balanced sampling.
- **No LLM judge** — `--no-judge` implied (no `--llm-alias` was passed). All scores are retrieval-only (`contains`, R@5, NDCG@5).
- **No comparison to published baselines** — these numbers are uniko's pipeline against this slice; not directly comparable to Memzero / A-Mem / etc. without controlled-config reruns.
- **xsmall FP32 is not the fastest NLP variant** under our current Option-1 SRL batching (small at 111 ms/msg beat xsmall at 140 ms/msg in standalone nlp-compare). xsmall as default here was a continuity choice with prior runs, not a perf-tuned choice.

## What this slice tells us is worth doing next

1. **SSA chunking investigation** — three different chunking parameters / strategies, measure `contains` against the same 3 SSA questions. Currently 33% suggests there's a real problem.
2. **MS recall depth** — the reranker improved 1 of 3 by reordering more candidates. Worth measuring `--reranker-top-n` 50 → 100 → 200 to see if a wider pool surfaces the missing evidence sessions on `6d550036`.
3. **A real 30-q sequential run** at `q=1` to get a higher-confidence aggregate; this 11-q slice is too noisy to commit a config change to.
